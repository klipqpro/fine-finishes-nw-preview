Overview

What cabinet refinishing actually is.

Cabinet refinishing means painting the cabinets you already have instead of buying new ones. We take the doors off, clean and sand everything, prime it, and spray on a new finish. Fine Finishes NW refinishes kitchen cabinets, bathroom vanities and built-in shelving in Vancouver, Camas, Ridgefield, Battle Ground and the Portland metro. Most kitchens take three to five days and cost far less than new cabinets.

Why refinish

Your cabinets are probably fine.

Most kitchens people want to tear out don't have anything wrong with them. The boxes are solid, the doors hang straight, the drawers still slide. What people don't like is the color. And color is paint, not carpentry.

New cabinets mean tearing out what you have, pulling off the countertop, moving plumbing and wiring, and living without a kitchen for weeks. Painting them fixes the part you actually don't like, in a few days, for a lot less money.

We'll also tell you when it isn't worth doing. If your boxes have water damage, if the surface is already peeling away from the wood, or if you don't like where things are, paint won't fix any of that. We'd rather tell you that at the estimate than take the job and leave you disappointed.

The process

How we do it, step by step.

Cabinets get touched, wiped and slammed more than anything else in your house. Skipping any one of these steps is why painted cabinets start chipping.

01

We seal off the room

Plastic sheeting over the doorways, covers on the floors, and a fan venting the dust outside. Sanding cabinets makes a lot of fine dust, and without this it ends up in every room in the house.

02

Doors come off and get numbered

Every door and drawer front is labeled so it goes back exactly where it came from. Hinges and handles are bagged and labeled by location, not dumped in one bucket to sort out later.

03

We wash off the grease

Kitchen cabinets collect cooking grease, especially around the stove. Paint will not stick to grease. So everything gets scrubbed with a degreaser before anything else happens.

04

We sand off the shine

Factory cabinets have a slick, shiny coating. We sand every surface dull so the primer has something to grab onto. This is the step cheap jobs skip, and it's why their paint peels off the edges.

05

We prime with a bonding primer

A bonding primer is made to stick to slick surfaces — ordinary wall primer won't hold on cabinets. If your cabinets are oak and you want a smooth modern look, this is where we fill in the grain.

06

We spray two coats

Two coats of a hard cabinet paint, sprayed on rather than brushed, with a light sanding in between. No brush marks, no roller texture, and a surface you can actually wipe clean.

What we paint

More than kitchens.

  • Kitchen cabinets. Uppers, lowers, islands and pantry doors, all finished to the same color and sheen so nothing looks patched together.
  • Bathroom vanities. A small job that changes the whole room, and a cheap way to see our work before you commit to a whole kitchen.
  • Built-in shelving. Bookcases, window seats, mudroom lockers and TV walls. If it can't come off the wall, we paint it in place.
  • Interior doors and trim. Worth doing at the same time, so the whites in the room actually match each other.
  • New cabinets for builders. Unfinished cabinets sprayed before or after they go in, as part of our new construction work.

Good questions

Cabinet questions, answered.

What people ask us at almost every kitchen estimate.

How long does it take?

Three to five working days for a normal kitchen, plus a little drying time before you put everything back in. We paint the doors and drawer fronts away from the kitchen, so for most of that week you can still use the room — you're just living without cabinet doors for a few days. Bigger kitchens take longer, and so does filling in oak grain. You get the schedule with your estimate.

Is painting cheaper than buying new cabinets?

Yes, by a lot, in almost every kitchen. With new cabinets you're paying for the cabinets themselves, plus tearing out the old ones, taking the countertop off and putting it back, disconnecting plumbing and wiring, and hauling everything away. Painting skips all of that. How much you save depends on the size of your kitchen and the shape your cabinets are in, which is why we give you a written price after seeing it rather than guessing over the phone.

Will the paint chip or peel?

Not if the prep was done right. Painted cabinets fail at the edges when someone painted over grease, didn't sand off the shine, or used regular wall primer instead of a bonding primer. We do all three of those steps, and the work comes with a written warranty. If something we did fails, we come back and fix it.

Can you paint oak cabinets so the grain doesn't show?

Yes. Oak has an open grain, so we fill it and sand it back before priming. That adds a day or two but gives you the smooth, modern look most people want. If you'd rather keep the grain showing as texture, we can do that instead — it's faster and cheaper. We'll paint a sample door both ways so you can see them in your own kitchen before deciding.

Can you match a specific color?

Yes. We can match a paint color code, a sample you bring us, or cabinets you already have if you're adding to them. We always paint a sample door and let you look at it in your own kitchen light first, because colors look very different under your kitchen lights than they do on a paint chip in the store.

Do you do rentals and apartment buildings?

Often. Painting cabinets turns a dated unit around much faster and cheaper than replacing them, which matters when the unit is sitting empty. We work with property managers on timing and can do several units in a row. See commercial painting for how that works.
