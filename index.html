08 — Good questions

Before you hire anyone.

The things people ask us most — answered straight, including the ones that make some contractors squirm.

How much does painting cost?

It depends on square footage, surface condition, how many colors, and how much prep and repair the surfaces need — anyone who quotes you a price over the phone without seeing the job is guessing. We come out, measure, look closely at the condition of your surfaces, and give you a written estimate that itemizes the scope so you can compare it fairly against anyone else's.

How long will my project take?

A typical interior repaint of a few rooms runs two to four days. A full house interior is usually one to two weeks; a full exterior, three days to two weeks depending on size, prep and weather. We give you a schedule with your estimate, and if anything changes we tell you the same day — not after the fact.

Do I need to move my furniture?

No. We move and cover it for you. Large or fragile pieces we'll talk through with you first, and we ask that you handle genuinely irreplaceable items yourself — not because we're careless, but because nobody should have to trust a stranger with those.

What kind of paint do you use?

Premium lines from the major manufacturers, chosen for the specific surface and exposure rather than whatever's cheapest that week. Northwest exteriors in particular need coatings rated for our wet winters — the wrong product will fail in a few seasons no matter how well it's applied.

Are you licensed and insured?

Yes — licensed, bonded and insured in both Washington and Oregon, and happy to send you the certificates before you commit to anything. If a contractor hesitates when you ask this, that's your answer about them.

Do you offer a warranty?

Yes. Every project carries a written workmanship warranty, and we tell you exactly what it covers and for how long before you sign. If something we did fails, we come back and fix it.

Can you paint in the winter?

Interiors, absolutely — winter is actually our best availability, and the work is easier to schedule. Exteriors need dry conditions and surface temperatures above roughly 50°F, so we watch the forecast closely and won't push a coating on in the wrong conditions just to keep a crew busy.

Do you work with builders on new construction?

Constantly — it's a big part of what we do. Nearly every project in our portfolio is a custom home we finished for a Northwest builder. If you're a builder looking for a finish partner who hits dates and doesn't create punch-list work for everyone else, start here.
